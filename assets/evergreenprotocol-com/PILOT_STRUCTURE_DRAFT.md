# Pilot Structure Draft — evergreenprotocol.com

## Purpose

This document is the internal draft of the pilot structure for qualified operating partners of Evergreen Protocol. It governs what terms may be discussed, what must remain fixed, and what requires formal written agreement before any operating activity begins.

This is a preparation and reference document for internal use. It does not constitute a binding agreement. No operating activity may begin on the basis of this document alone. All formal terms require explicit written agreement between the operator and Sohadot before any engagement commences.

---

## Pilot Type

Non-exclusive operating pilot.

No pilot arrangement grants exclusive operating rights in any sector, geography, or service category. Multiple operators may hold concurrent pilot arrangements across different service lines or delivery contexts, at Sohadot's discretion and without disclosure obligation.

---

## Duration Options

Pilot arrangements may be structured in two durations:

**6-month pilot**
Appropriate for operators testing a single service line or bounded initial deployment. Suitable for advisory firms or platform operators beginning with a single offer type.

**12-month pilot**
Appropriate for operators with broader service line coverage, more complex institutional delivery models, or a longer sales cycle in their target client base.

Duration is confirmed in the formal written agreement. Extension or renewal is subject to performance review and Sohadot governance approval at the end of the initial pilot term. Extension is not automatic.

---

## Domain and Protocol Control

The following remain under exclusive Sohadot control throughout any pilot arrangement and for all time thereafter:

- The evergreenprotocol.com domain and all associated DNS, hosting, and publication rights
- The Evergreen Protocol category standard, its version history, its scope, and its governed terms
- The machine-readable reference files and their update, versioning, and publication governance
- The public changelog, version governance cadence, and review records
- The right to approve or reject public claims referencing the protocol, including marketing materials, service descriptions, and case studies
- The right to approve or reject derivative services, materials, presentations, or communications referencing the protocol
- The right to modify the public reference surface, the category standard, or any governed document without operator consent

No pilot arrangement, and no term within a pilot arrangement, transfers domain ownership, protocol ownership, category standard governance, or any form of intellectual property right to the operator.

Control terms are non-negotiable and apply for the full pilot duration and after.

---

## Operator Commercialization Scope

During a pilot, the operator may commercialize the following approved service types under governed boundaries:

- Reference Continuity Review
- AI Documentation Continuity Review
- Knowledge System Continuity Review
- Protocol Continuity Brief
- Enterprise Readiness Workshop
- Internal Governance Documentation Review

All client-facing descriptions of these services must be reviewed and approved by Sohadot before delivery to clients or publication in marketing materials.

The operator may not, during or after the pilot:
- Issue certifications, badges, scores, ratings, or compliance statuses referencing the protocol
- Create or operate a registry of assessed, reviewed, or certified organizations
- Develop and publish derivative standards, derivative protocol documentation, or derivative reference frameworks
- Alter or restate the public category standard, its scope, or its governed terms
- Make public claims about the protocol not explicitly approved in writing by Sohadot
- Sub-license operating rights to third parties without explicit written approval

New service types not listed above must be submitted to Sohadot for written approval before commercialization.

## Revenue Share Framework

Revenue share from collected operating revenue may be discussed and agreed as part of the formal pilot arrangement.

No specific percentage or financial term is established in this document. Revenue share terms are agreed in the formal written pilot agreement and confirmed before any commercialization activity begins.

Revenue share applies to operating revenue collected by the operator from services that directly reference, are built on, or are marketed in connection with the Evergreen Protocol framework. Revenue share terms do not apply to the operator's existing revenue from service lines unrelated to the protocol.

The economic order governing Sohadot's revenue participation in any pilot arrangement:

1. Authority preservation — no revenue arrangement may degrade the protocol's reference integrity or institutional positioning
2. Reference integrity — no revenue arrangement may require certification, scoring, or registry behavior to generate revenue
3. Dignified recurring income — revenue participation is structured to reflect the protocol's governed status
4. Long-horizon positioning quality — revenue terms must not create expectations or incentives that conflict with the asset's long-horizon acquisition quality

---

## Public Claims Governance

All public claims referencing the protocol are subject to prior written approval from Sohadot:

- Client-facing marketing materials, service pages, and proposal templates referencing Evergreen Protocol must be reviewed by Sohadot before use
- Service descriptions using the protocol name must conform to the permitted claim boundaries in `OUTREACH_CLAIM_BOUNDARY.md`
- Press releases, case studies, testimonials, or public announcements referencing the protocol require explicit written approval before publication
- Website or platform language referencing the protocol as a basis for services must be approved before going live
- Conference or event presentations referencing the protocol must be reviewed before delivery

The operator is responsible for ensuring that all team members, subcontractors, and client-facing representatives understand and operate within the claim boundaries. Claim boundary violations during the pilot period are grounds for termination of the pilot arrangement.

---

## Post-Traction Path

A successful pilot creates a basis — not a right or a guarantee — for the following post-traction discussions:

**Institutional license**
Broader, more durable operating rights under formally agreed terms, including potential sector-specific scope and a longer license horizon.

**Sector-specific operating rights**
Formal rights to operate within a defined market segment, service category, or geographic region under agreed conditions.

**Post-traction acquisition discussion**
An invitation to discuss potential acquisition of the domain and protocol. This is a discussion, not a commitment. No acquisition right is granted by pilot participation, pilot completion, revenue generation, or any other operating activity.

"Demonstrated traction" means the operator has successfully delivered at least one commercialized service built on the protocol framework during the pilot period, with documented client engagement and collected revenue.

All post-traction discussions are initiated at Sohadot's sole discretion. The operator may not initiate acquisition discussion as a contractual right.

---

## Termination

Either party may terminate the pilot arrangement with written notice under the terms confirmed in the formal written agreement. Upon termination:

- The operator immediately ceases all commercialization of services referencing the protocol
- All client-facing marketing materials, service descriptions, and public content referencing the protocol are removed or corrected within the timeline agreed in the formal agreement
- Revenue share obligations for services delivered during the pilot period continue to apply per the formal agreement terms
- No post-traction discussions are initiated or continued after termination unless separately agreed in writing

---

## Formalization Requirements

No operating activity may begin until all of the following are confirmed in writing:

1. Formal written pilot agreement signed by both parties
2. Written approval of the operator's initial service descriptions and offer language
3. Written approval of the operator's public claims and client-facing marketing materials
4. Confirmation of revenue share terms
5. Confirmation that the operator has reviewed and accepts the claim boundary document

Informal discussion of the pilot structure — including in calls, emails, or meetings — does not constitute authorization to begin operating, marketing, or delivering services referencing the protocol.
