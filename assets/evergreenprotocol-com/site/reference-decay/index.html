Reference problem layer · Core term

Reference Decay

Reference decay is the loss of validity, traceability, citation stability, or operational reliability of a digital reference over time, even when the reference remains accessible.

Definition

A digital reference — a policy, definition, protocol, standard, model record, knowledge-base entry, or governance document — decays when it loses one or more of the properties that made it authoritative at the point it was trusted:

  • It no longer reflects the current policy, decision, or operational reality it was intended to describe.
  • It can no longer be cited at a specific version to distinguish what it said at a given point in time.
  • Its chain of evidence from source to current state is broken or untraceable.
  • Downstream systems, agents, or users continue to rely on it as valid when it is not.

Reference decay does not require deletion or inaccessibility. A reference can load, appear authoritative, and continue to be cited while no longer being operationally valid. This is what makes it difficult to detect and govern.

Why it matters

Reference decay is an institutional risk problem, not only a documentation problem. When a reference decays silently:

  • Decisions built on it inherit the decay. If the reference that justified an action was already invalid, the decision’s evidence foundation cannot be reconstructed.
  • AI agents and automated workflows that consume the reference propagate the decay at scale and speed, without a human review step that might otherwise detect the problem.
  • Compliance and audit functions that depend on citing the governing reference at the point of a decision cannot establish that the citation was valid at that moment.
  • Institutional knowledge built on decayed references accumulates contradiction, ambiguity, and untraceable provenance over time.

The compound effect across an institution is a governance layer that appears complete but can no longer be fully trusted, cited, or verified.

Why AI agents make it urgent

Enterprise AI agents — deployed across knowledge management, compliance, finance, supply chain, legal operations, and customer advisory — act on institutional references. They consume policies, definitions, documentation, data dictionaries, compliance notes, model records, and knowledge bases to produce outputs, take actions, and support decisions.

The governance challenge this creates is distinct from model governance:

Model governance asks whether the model is safe. Reference continuity asks whether the references the model acts on are still valid.

A well-governed, high-quality AI agent producing outputs from a decayed reference is a governed model operating on ungoverned material. The model may meet every safety and fairness standard while the institutional knowledge it depends on has silently become invalid, outdated, or contradictory.

This gap is not addressed by model evaluation, safety testing, or deployment governance alone. It requires reference continuity governance: the discipline of keeping the references that AI agents act on valid, versioned, citable, and governed across time.

Common forms of reference decay

  • Silent policy edits. A policy page is updated in place with no version record. Prior citations to the policy no longer reference what they cited at the point of use.
  • Unversioned model documentation. A model card or AI system record is revised without preserving the prior version. The history of what the system was evaluated against is lost.
  • Outdated knowledge-base entries. An AI assistant cites a knowledge-base article after the policy owner has changed the rule the article described. The article remains accessible and appears authoritative.
  • Broken citation paths. External standards, regulatory references, or technical specifications that internal documents depend on change location, version, or content without the internal document registering the change.
  • Definitions changing without a changelog. A term used in governance documents shifts meaning across teams or versions without a controlled update. The bounded meaning the term carried at the point of use is no longer recoverable.
  • Evidence records losing context. Audit-facing outputs, advisory summaries, and decision records cannot be traced back to the source material that was valid when the output was produced.
  • Machine-readable files diverging from human pages. A JSON reference file and its human-readable counterpart are updated independently. Consumers that depend on either diverge in what they treat as authoritative.

Reference decay versus model risk

Reference decay is not the same as model risk, and addressing model risk does not address reference decay:

Model risk asks
Whether the model behaves safely and fairly. Addressed by evaluation, red-teaming, and deployment governance. Failure mode: the model produces harmful or inaccurate outputs. Typically visible in model outputs and behavior.
Reference decay asks
Whether the material the model acts on remains valid. Addressed by version discipline, citation stability, and review cadence. Failure mode: the model produces governed outputs from invalid premises. Often invisible — the output looks correct, the reference looks valid.

Existing AI governance frameworks — model cards, safety evaluations, responsible AI principles, risk tiering — address model risk. Reference decay requires a distinct and complementary governance layer.

Evergreen Protocol reading

Evergreen Protocol reads reference decay through six governance dimensions. Each dimension corresponds to a property that, when absent or degraded, produces a recognizable form of reference decay:

  • Version discipline. Material changes to references are recorded with version, date, authority, and rationale. Without this, prior and current reference states cannot be distinguished.
  • Citation stability. References can be cited at stable, version-specific locations. Without this, citations decay as the referenced content changes or moves.
  • Evidence posture. Decisions can be traced to evidence that was valid at the point of use. Without this, institutional decisions lose their provenance.
  • Definition control. Governed terms have bounded, versioned, citable definitions. Without this, the meaning of key terms drifts silently across documents.
  • Changelog integrity. A trusted change record exists for material reference changes. Without this, institutional memory depends on individual recall rather than governed record.
  • Machine-readable reference structure. Reference data is consumable by AI agents and institutional systems from stable, versioned files. Without this, machine consumers depend on unstable scraping or inference.

This reading does not assess, certify, or score any institution’s reference posture. It names the governance dimensions whose absence produces reference decay.

What this page does not claim

This page defines a term and explains a governance problem. It does not:

  • Certify or assess any organization’s reference continuity posture.
  • Issue scores, ratings, maturity levels, or compliance designations.
  • Claim that Evergreen Protocol eliminates reference decay — it governs a discipline, not an outcome guarantee.
  • Assert that reference decay is not addressed by any other framework — adjacent governance frameworks address related problems from different angles.
  • Open a registry, directory, or certification program of any kind.
