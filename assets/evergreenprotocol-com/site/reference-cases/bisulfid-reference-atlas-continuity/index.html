Self-applied reference case · reference atlas continuity

Bisulfid — Reference Atlas Continuity

This case documents how Evergreen Protocol principles have been applied to govern the reference surface integrity, source discipline, indexability, and long-horizon terminology stability of the Bisulfid reference atlas.

Case Type

Reference atlas continuity. A large, multi-entry governed reference surface where each entry must meet source discipline, content substance, and terminology consistency standards to remain citable and discoverable over time.

Continuity Problem

A reference atlas that expands across a large number of entries faces compounding continuity risks that are different in character from single-topic documentation:

  • Source traceability drift — as entries accumulate, individual citations can lose their link to the original authoritative source if source governance is not maintained explicitly at the entry level.
  • Sitemap and indexability decay — without governed thresholds for what enters the public index, thin entries can accumulate and degrade the atlas’s standing as a reliable reference surface.
  • Thin-page proliferation — stub entries without sufficient governed reference content reduce the citability of the atlas and create reference decay across the entire surface.
  • Terminology drift — when different entries handle adjacent compound classes or related categories without a shared controlled vocabulary, terminology diverges silently across the atlas.
  • Silent update problem — when entries are revised without a public record of the change, the atlas loses its claim to reference continuity; readers and AI agents cannot detect when the content they cited has changed.

Each of these problems compounds the others. Source drift produces thin pages; thin pages exit the effective index; terminology drift means adjacent entries cannot be cited together coherently; silent updates mean the entire surface becomes untrustworthy as a stable citation target.

Evergreen Protocol Principles Demonstrated

  • Bounded scope — the atlas declares which compound classes and reference domains it covers and explicitly excludes adjacent domains such as safety data, regulatory compliance, or industrial applications. Scope boundaries prevent reference inflation and keep the atlas citable within its governed territory.
  • Source discipline — each entry traces to at least one authoritative external source; entries without traceable sources are excluded from the public index until source governance requirements are met.
  • Sitemap governance — pages are admitted to the public sitemap only when they meet reference substance thresholds; thin or stub entries are withheld from indexing until they qualify.
  • Controlled vocabulary — a shared set of governed terms covers compound categories, property labels, and relationship descriptors across all atlas entries, preventing the silent terminology divergence that degrades cross-reference citability.
  • Public change memory — when an entry is updated, a visible revision record is maintained; readers and systems that cited the entry can detect that the content has changed and re-verify as needed.
  • Review cadence — entries are reviewed on a cadence tied to source availability and field-level change rate, not arbitrary intervals; entries whose sources have been superseded are flagged for review.

What This Case Does Not Claim

  • This case does not certify Bisulfid as compliant with any external scientific or reference standard.
  • This case does not evaluate or score the atlas against other reference surfaces.
  • This case does not establish Evergreen Protocol as an authority over chemistry reference standards or nomenclature.
  • This case does not validate the scientific accuracy of any specific entry within the atlas.
  • This case does not create any compliance status, approval, or external recognition for the asset.
