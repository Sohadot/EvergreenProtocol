Reference Continuity Evidence Brief

A proposed paid pilot for qualified institutional teams that need a traceable account of whether the references behind a documented process remain identifiable, versioned, and citable as that process changes.

The operating question

A policy, model card, procedural rule, or knowledge-base entry may still have a working URL while its meaning, version, owner, or relationship to other sources has changed. A reference continuity review examines the evidence trail behind such changes. It does not determine whether an AI system is safe, whether a legal duty has been met, or whether an organization is compliant.

Proposed first-pilot boundary

  • One reference chain: one bounded institutional workflow or documentation question, agreed before any work begins.
  • Up to ten sources: public URLs or other explicitly authorized, publicly available reference artifacts supplied or approved by the requesting organization.
  • Review dimensions: version provenance, citation stability, definition consistency, named ownership where public, dependency links, and declared review cadence.
  • Evidence discipline: dated source inventory; each observation tied to the available record; unavailable evidence and unverified assumptions identified rather than guessed.

Private, regulated, or confidential materials are outside this first public-evidence pilot. A private-document review would require a separately agreed secure process and appropriate professional capacity.

What a completed brief would contain

  1. A source register stating the reviewed URL or artifact, observed version where available, and date of review.
  2. An evidence-linked findings table distinguishing confirmed observations, ambiguities, and information not available.
  3. A reference dependency map showing where one document, definition, or decision relies on another.
  4. Bounded recommendations for version discipline, citation continuity, and document ownership, with limitations stated.

No numerical readiness score, certification badge, compliance opinion, independent endorsement, or guarantee of risk elimination is produced.

How a qualified engagement may proceed

  1. Scope discussion: identify the institutional decision and the public reference chain to examine.
  2. Written proposal: confirm permitted sources, delivery capacity, reviewer roles, timing, fee, ownership, and exclusions.
  3. Acceptance: work begins only after both parties agree to the scope and commercial terms.
  4. Evidence review: prepare the bounded brief and make uncertainty visible.
  5. Delivery and feedback: review the usefulness of the brief; no client result is published without permission.

There is no live checkout, automated assessment, public client registry, or currently active certification program. Publishing this proposed engagement does not itself establish an operating pilot or a paying customer.
