Market context

AI-Era Digital Continuity

AI-era acceleration does not only create new capabilities. It creates new forms of institutional fragility — specifically, the decay of reference systems that organizations depend on to make decisions, demonstrate compliance, and maintain coherent knowledge over time.

What reference decay looks like

Reference decay is not a single catastrophic failure. It is a slow structural erosion that becomes visible only when it is already costly to reverse. It presents as:

  • Documentation that no one trusts, but no one has formally invalidated.
  • Policies that reference systems or roles that no longer exist.
  • AI-generated outputs whose governing context has shifted, making prior outputs unverifiable.
  • Definitions used inconsistently across teams because no versioned, citable definition exists.
  • Decisions that cannot be traced to the protocols in force at the time they were made.
  • Knowledge bases that reflect the organizational structure of two years ago, not today.
  • Protocols that exist in draft, in multiple versions, with no authority record of which is current.

In each case, the underlying resource exists. But its reference integrity has failed. It can no longer be trusted, cited, or operationally relied upon.

Why AI-era conditions make this acute

Reference decay predates AI. What AI-era conditions have changed is the rate and surface area of the problem.

  • Model change velocity. AI models and their outputs change faster than organizations update the governance context those outputs operate within. A policy governing AI-assisted decisions may be technically valid but practically obsolete within months.
  • Team composition fluidity. AI-augmented workflows accelerate team restructuring. Institutional knowledge embedded in people — rather than governed references — evaporates faster.
  • Documentation inflation. AI systems produce documentation at high volume. The governance of which documents are authoritative, versioned, and citable cannot scale at the same rate through informal practice.
  • Compliance surface expansion. Regulatory frameworks for AI are active and evolving. Organizations need reference systems that can absorb regulatory change without losing traceability of prior states.
  • Agent and system interdependency. AI agents operating on behalf of institutions require precise, versioned, citable context. Reference systems that decay silently produce incorrect, unverifiable agent behavior with no traceable governance trail.

The governance gap

Existing governance frameworks address parts of this landscape effectively. Security frameworks govern information integrity. Compliance frameworks govern regulatory adherence. AI risk frameworks govern model behavior and deployment. Knowledge management systems govern information architecture.

The specific governance gap is: no established standard defines how institutional reference systems themselves — the protocols, definitions, evidence structures, and knowledge assets that underpin all other governance — maintain continuity under change.

Evergreen Protocol addresses this gap through the category of reference continuity governance: a structured, citable, versioned approach to keeping institutional reference systems valid and operationally coherent over time.

What reference continuity governance provides

A reference system governed under the Evergreen Protocol standard provides:

  • Citeability. Any reference, protocol, definition, or decision basis can be cited at the version in force at a specific point in time.
  • Traceability. Changes to reference systems are recorded, authored, and attributed — creating a governance trail.
  • Review governance. Reference systems are assessed for continued validity on a defined cadence, not left to decay silently.
  • Boundary clarity. Each protocol states what it governs and what it does not — preventing scope drift and silent expansion.
  • Evidence posture. Organizations can demonstrate, to internal or external reviewers, that their reference systems were valid at the point of use.
