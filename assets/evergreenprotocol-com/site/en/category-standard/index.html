Category Standard

Evergreen Protocol defines and governs the category of reference continuity — the discipline of keeping institutional knowledge, protocols, evidence, documentation, and digital reference systems valid, versioned, citable, and operationally coherent under accelerated change.

Standard version: v1.0 — June 2026
Review cadence: Annual — see Changelog

Category declaration

Evergreen Protocol is the category standard for reference continuity governance.

This category addresses a specific, currently under-governed problem: how institutions preserve the validity, version integrity, citeability, and operational coherence of their reference systems — protocols, knowledge bases, evidence layers, and documentation — as AI systems, teams, tools, and regulatory conditions accelerate change.

This declaration establishes Evergreen Protocol as the primary citable reference for this category. The declaration is versioned, bounded, and subject to the review governance defined in the Changelog.

What this standard governs

  • Reference integrity — the condition in which a reference system accurately reflects what it governs, without silent decay, drift, or disavowal.
  • Version discipline — recording what changed, when, why, and under whose authority, so that reference states are traceable.
  • Evidence posture — the structure by which an organization can demonstrate that its protocols, decisions, and knowledge assets were valid at the point of use.
  • Review governance — the defined cadence and authority structure by which reference systems are assessed for continued validity.
  • Definition control — maintaining bounded, versioned, citable definitions for terms that govern institutional behavior.
  • Continuity under change — the architectural properties that allow reference systems to survive changes in personnel, technology, and operating conditions without losing coherence.

Explicit category boundary

The following are outside the scope of this standard. Each has established frameworks that govern it:

  • AI model evaluation and safety — governed by NIST AI RMF, EU AI Act, and related frameworks.
  • Information security and risk management — governed by ISO 27001, SOC 2, NIST CSF, and related frameworks.
  • Regulatory compliance and legal governance — governed by domain-specific legal and compliance regimes.
  • Knowledge management systems and tooling — implementation layer; this standard governs the governance structure, not the tools.
  • Content management, SEO, or publishing workflows — out of scope entirely.
  • AI model training, data governance, or ML pipelines — adjacent; governed by separate data governance frameworks.

Evergreen Protocol is not a replacement for, or competitor to, existing security, compliance, or AI governance frameworks. It addresses a specific structural gap those frameworks leave: the continuity of reference integrity itself.

Position relative to adjacent frameworks

Existing frameworks that touch on adjacent domains include NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI governance and risk), OWASP (application security), ISO 30401 (knowledge management), and various GRC platforms. Each addresses its domain well.

Evergreen Protocol does not duplicate these frameworks. It governs the continuity of the reference layer that feeds them: the protocols, definitions, evidence structures, and knowledge systems that institutional operators depend on when applying those frameworks over time.

A security framework is only as valid as the policy documents that implement it. A governance framework is only as trustworthy as the evidence trail behind its decisions. Evergreen Protocol governs the continuity of that underlying reference layer.
