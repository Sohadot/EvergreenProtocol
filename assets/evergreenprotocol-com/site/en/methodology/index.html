Structural layer

Methodology

Operational template: formal shape, proof artifacts, validity-driven cadence, controlled change.

Three layers in this document
  • Architecture of Constancy — Sections 1–4 and 7.
  • Anti-Entropy Protocol — Section 6.
  • Zero-Season Algorithm — Section 5.

This document specifies how to construct an Evergreen Protocol as defined by this reference. It implements the Architecture of Constancy (structure), is motivated by the Anti-Entropy Protocol (necessity), and encodes the Zero-Season Algorithm as review cadence and entropy checks.

1. Protocol charter

Every Evergreen Protocol begins with a one-page charter containing:

  • Title and identifier — a stable name and a short code (e.g., EP-AUTH-01).
  • Purpose — one paragraph stating the outcome the protocol protects (validity of decisions, integrity of definitions, continuity of a service, etc.).
  • Scope boundary — explicit inclusions and exclusions. Exclusions must name adjacent domains handled elsewhere.
  • Primary risk addressed — the failure mode if the protocol did not exist (drift, silent exceptions, ambiguous ownership, stale public surfaces).
  • Success definition — what “compliant” means in observable terms, not aspirational adjectives.

2. Roles and decision rights

Assign named roles. Titles may vary by organization; functions may not.

  • Maintainer — keeps the protocol text current, coordinates reviews, maintains the change log.
  • Approver — authorizes material changes and exceptions.
  • Evidence steward — ensures artifacts exist, are reachable, and match the protocol’s claims.
  • Consumer — roles or teams that must follow the protocol in operation; they have a right to readable specs and predictable change windows.

Decision rights must state: who may propose, who must consult, who approves, and what happens on disagreement (escalation path).

3. Artifacts

Artifacts are the proof layer. Each protocol lists:

  • Authoritative text — the protocol document version.
  • Registers — tables of exceptions, dependencies, owners, or data definitions, as applicable.
  • Interfaces — URLs, APIs, forms, or system screens governed by the protocol.
  • Evidence samples — examples of compliant records (redacted as needed).

If an artifact cannot be pointed to, it cannot be audited. The protocol must be revised until artifacts are concrete.

4. Acceptance criteria

Acceptance criteria translate intent into checks. Each criterion should be testable. Examples:

  • “Every public page in scope lists an owner and last-reviewed date.”
  • “Schema field X has a single definition in the register; duplicates are treated as defects.”
  • “Exceptions require Approver sign-off and expire on a stated date.”

5. Review cadence (Zero-Season Algorithm)

Define a calendar of validity, not a calendar of fashion. Minimum structure:

  • Baseline review — scheduled interval (e.g., annual) triggered by time.
  • Event-triggered review — mandatory when named dependencies change (vendor, regulation, security class, data source).
  • Quiet period rule — optional: blackout windows where only emergency fixes apply, to prevent churn disguised as progress.

Reviews answer: Is the protocol still true? Are artifacts still aligned? Are exceptions still justified?

6. Entropy checks (Anti-Entropy Protocol)

Each review includes a short entropy checklist:

  • Tacit exceptions — Have new “special cases” appeared without documentation?
  • Definition fork — Do two teams use the same term with different meanings?
  • Surface drift — Do public or client-facing interfaces contradict the authoritative text?
  • Ownership vacuum — Is any artifact without a named steward?

Findings are recorded; material findings trigger a version bump or an exception process.

7. Versioning and change control

Use semantic clarity, not vanity numbering.

  • Major — scope, roles, or acceptance criteria change in a way that alters obligations.
  • Minor — clarifications that do not change obligations but reduce ambiguity.
  • Patch — typographical fixes, link repairs, or non-semantic corrections.

Each release has: version number, date, author, summary, and link to diff or change log.

8. Mapping the three layers to this template

  • Architecture of Constancy — Sections 1–4 and 7: what stays formally stable across time.
  • Anti-Entropy Protocol — Sections 5–6: resistance to unnecessary drift.
  • Zero-Season Algorithm — Section 5: review timing tied to validity, not spectacle.

9. Worked example (illustrative, non-prescriptive)

Title: Evergreen Protocol for Public Definition of “Customer Record”

  • Scope: Defines fields and meanings for “Customer Record” used in customer-facing dashboards; excludes legal contracts and billing tax logic.
  • Roles: Maintainer = data governance lead; Approver = head of product; Evidence steward = analytics engineer.
  • Artifacts: Definition register (table), dashboard lineage doc, sample dashboard with owner footer.
  • Acceptance: No dashboard ships without mapping each displayed metric to a register row ID.
  • Review cadence: Annual baseline; event-triggered on warehouse migration or metric rename.
  • Entropy checks: Search for duplicate metric names; verify owner footers; scan for undocumented calculated fields.

Note. This example demonstrates shape, not sector doctrine. Replace entities, fields, and roles to fit your environment. The requirement is structural completeness, not domain specificity.

10. Controlled vocabulary

Terms used here are defined in Definitions. Protocol authors must not redefine those terms locally without explicit exception.
