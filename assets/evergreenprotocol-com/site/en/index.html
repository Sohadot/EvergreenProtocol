Category standard · v1.0

Digital systems do not only fail when they go offline.
They fail when their references decay.

Evergreen Protocol is the citable category standard for preserving reference integrity, evidence posture, version discipline, and institutional knowledge continuity under accelerated digital change.

Reading path:
Overview → Category Standard → AI-Era Continuity → Methodology
Audience: Institutional operators, AI governance leads, GRC practitioners, digital infrastructure teams

The problem this standard addresses

Institutional knowledge decays. Protocols go stale. Documentation loses its reference integrity. AI-assisted systems accelerate the problem: models change, outputs shift, and the organizational context that made a decision valid disappears without trace.

Existing governance frameworks address compliance, security, and risk. The specific problem of reference continuity — keeping knowledge, evidence, and protocols valid, versioned, citable, and operationally coherent over time — remains under-governed.

Evergreen Protocol addresses that gap.

What this standard defines

An Evergreen Protocol is a documented unit of governance with four non-negotiable properties:

  • Bounded scope — states what it governs and what it explicitly does not govern.
  • Named roles — assigns who may initiate change, who approves, and who maintains evidence.
  • Artifacts — lists the records, interfaces, or documents that constitute proof of compliance.
  • Review and versioning — defines when the protocol is revisited and how changes are recorded.

If one of these properties is missing, the document may be useful, but it is not operating as an Evergreen Protocol within this reference model.

Conceptual hierarchy

The standard operates through three ordered layers. The structural layer defines what can be built; the philosophical layer justifies why it must exist; the symbolic layer elevates meaning without replacing formal clarity.

Structural layer

Architecture of Constancy

Stable shape of continuity: charters, boundaries, decision rights, artifacts, acceptance criteria, and change control. Operational spine of the Methodology.

Philosophical layer

Anti-Entropy Protocol

Necessity: drift — ambiguous policy, inconsistent definitions, tacit exceptions, interface decay — is normal; unnecessary drift is addressed through explicit protocols, not through claims of perfect control.

Symbolic layer

Zero-Season Algorithm

Review rhythms tied to validity and risk, not fashion cycles or arbitrary urgency — expressed as review cadence and entropy checks in practice.

What this standard is not

  • Not a compliance certification or external audit scheme.
  • Not a security or risk management framework — adjacent frameworks govern those domains.
  • Not a knowledge management system or content management methodology.
  • Not an AI model evaluation or safety tool.
  • Not a sector-specific playbook — sector applications belong under the applications layer, not the standard's identity.
