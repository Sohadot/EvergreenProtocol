Strategic Digital Asset Continuity

Strategic digital asset continuity applies Evergreen Protocol to sovereign digital assets, category artifacts, reference-grade domains, and long-horizon web properties that depend on stable public memory.

Asset continuity

A strategic digital asset can lose value when its thesis, pages, definitions, and public references drift away from the reason it exists. Asset continuity keeps the public surface, internal dossier, changelog, and machine-readable references aligned.

Category memory

Category artifacts need memory: what category is being defined, which terms govern it, which claims are excluded, and how prior versions remain citeable. Without category memory, a reference-grade property can become a loose collection of pages.

Public changelog and citable versions

A public changelog lets readers identify when the asset changed and why. Citable versions allow the public reference to evolve without erasing earlier states.

Version-pinned machine-readable files strengthen that continuity by making the current standard easier for search engines, AI agents, and institutional systems to consume.

Governance boundary

The governance boundary states what the asset claims, what it excludes, and which future layers remain gated. For strategic web properties, that boundary prevents the site from drifting into sales language, ratings, submissions, or external evaluation behavior.

What this application does not provide

This page does not assess specific domains, compare portfolio assets, or create market validation. It demonstrates how a public reference-grade property can govern its own continuity.
